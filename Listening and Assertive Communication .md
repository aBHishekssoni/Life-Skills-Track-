# Listening and Assertive Communication 


## What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?

### - Avoid getting distracted by your thoughts.
### - Do not try to interrupt the other person while they are speaking. 
### - Use door openers that shows you are interested.
### - When the other person speaks, show it with your body language too.
### - If appropriate, take notes during the conversation.
### - Paraphrase what the other person said to show you're on the same page.




## According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening? 

### - Focus and remove distractions
### - Use thoughtful silence, not idle chatter
### - Listen more, talk less
### - Respond to personal, not abstract, points
### - Understand the speaker’s perspective
### - Respond with empathy and acceptance
### - Notice tone and body language
### - Stay on the speaker’s specific points




## What are the obstacles in your listening process?

### - Distractions: phone, noise, notifications, other tabs
### - Thinking of a reply: preparing what to say instead of listening
### - Interrupting: finishing the speaker’s sentences or cutting in
### - Assumptions and bias: judging the speaker or topic too early
### - Lack of interest: the topic feels boring or irrelevant
### - Tiredness or stress: low energy, hunger, worry
### - Information overload: too much information at once
### - Language or accent issues: missing words or meaning
### - Ignoring non-verbal cues: missing tone, expression, body language
### - Emotional reactions: getting defensive or upset by what is said
### - Daydreaming: the mind drifts because we think faster than people speak




## What can you do to improve your listening?

### - Remove distractions
### - Give full attention
### - Don’t interrupt
### - Paraphrase what you heard
### - Ask clarifying questions
### - Notice tone and body language
### - Avoid early judgment
### - Practice regularly




## When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day to day life?

### - Talking to seniors or authority figures (mentor, manager, teacher), and staying quiet even when you disagree
### - Fear of conflict or rejection: saying “it’s okay” when it isn’t
### - Group settings: not sharing ideas because others seem more experienced
### - Saying yes when you want to say no: taking extra work or favors
### - Not asking for help or clarification: worrying about looking less capable
### - Low confidence or being new: in a new team, class, or workplace
### - Wanting to keep the peace: with friends or family





##  When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day to day life?

### - Stress or frustration: when overloaded, tired, or under deadline pressure
### - Feeling ignored or disrespected: raising your voice to be heard
### - Repeated mistakes by others: snapping at someone who keeps making the same error
### - Arguments or disagreements: interrupting, blaming, or using sarcasm
### - Bottled-up emotions: staying passive for a long time, then exploding
### - Feeling threatened or criticized: becoming defensive and attacking back
### - Traffic, queues, or delays: losing patience with strangers
### - Competitive situations: pushing your point to “win”





## When do you switch into Passive Aggressive (sarcasm/gossiping/taunts/silent treatment and others) communication styles in your day to day life?

### - Upset but avoiding confrontation: showing anger indirectly instead of saying it openly
### - Feeling unheard or unappreciated: using sarcasm (“Wow, great job...”) to make a point
### - Disagreeing with a senior or authority: complying outwardly but doing the task slowly or half-heartedly
### - Hurt by a friend: giving the silent treatment or short replies (“fine”, “whatever”)
### - Group conflicts: gossiping or complaining to others instead of talking to the person
### - Jealousy or resentment: making taunts or backhanded compliments
### - Fear of saying “no”: agreeing, then “forgetting” or delaying the task
### - Unspoken expectations: hoping others will guess what is wrong
